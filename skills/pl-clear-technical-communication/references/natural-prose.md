# Natural prose

Use this reference when editing prose, shortening text, or removing patterns that make writing sound artificial. Edit for meaning first, then for economy.

- Remove empty introductions, praise, repeated summaries, forced groups of three, synonym cycling, generic conclusions, and phrases such as "It is important to note that". Replace "utilize" with "use" when the meaning is the same.
- Name a source when attribution matters. Remove appeals to unnamed experts.
- State the mechanism, not a feeling: "The compiler reports a type error when the column name changes" is more useful than "The types follow your schema seamlessly."
- A list of overused words is a warning signal, not a ban. Keep technical nouns such as vector, primitive, or API surface when they carry an established meaning.
- Prefer active voice when the actor matters. Passive voice is fine when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Split a sentence when it is hard to parse, not merely because it has several clauses. Keep articles, verbs, and necessary qualifications; fragments and arrows can make short text harder to read.
- Use lists for real sets or steps and headings for distinct sections. Do not force a fixed number of items. Avoid excessive bold text and decorative emojis. Spanish quotation marks and punctuation take precedence over an English straight-quote preference.
- When editing the user's text, keep its meaning and facts. Do not add verification claims, and do not make an uncertain statement sound certain to shorten it.
