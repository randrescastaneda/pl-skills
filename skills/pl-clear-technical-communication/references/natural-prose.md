# Natural prose and anti-slop

Edit for meaning first, then economy. Prefer a direct answer, complete sentences, and coherent paragraphs. Keep the logical connection between cause, condition, and consequence.

Remove empty introductions, praise, repeated summaries, forced groups of three, synonym cycling, generic conclusions, and phrases such as "It is important to note that". Replace "utilize" with "use" when the meaning is the same. Name a source when attribution matters; otherwise remove unsupported appeals to unnamed experts.

State the mechanism rather than a feeling: "The compiler reports a type error when the column name changes" is more useful than "The types follow your schema seamlessly." Preserve technical nouns such as vector, primitive, or API surface when they carry an established meaning. A word list is a warning signal, not a prohibition.

Prefer active voice when it clarifies the actor. Passive voice is useful when the actor is unknown or irrelevant. Split a dense sentence when it is hard to parse, not merely because it has multiple clauses. Keep articles, verbs, and necessary qualifications; fragments and arrows can make short prose harder to understand.

Use lists for real sets or steps and headings for distinct sections. Do not impose a fixed number of items. Use punctuation normally when it improves clarity, subject to higher-priority style rules. Avoid excessive bold formatting and decorative emojis. Spanish quotation marks and punctuation take precedence over an English straight-quote preference.

For editing tasks, preserve the supplied meaning and facts. Do not add verification claims or make an uncertain statement sound certain merely to shorten it.
